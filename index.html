1971 — the catalytic event source stage I

Empty Chairs Don McLean, live at the Troubadour, Los Angeles

A solo, sparse performance — slow harmonic rhythm, chords in service of the text rather than calling attention to themselves. This is the witnessed occurrence, logged before it is compressed into anyone's song.

1971 — first compression source stage I → III

Killing Me Softly with His Song Lori Lieberman, debut album, Capitol Records

Written on a napkin during McLean's set, handed to Norman Gimbel that night, set to music by Charles Fox. Diatonic, folk-rooted, confessional — the melody as Fox composed it; the words as Gimbel shaped them from Lieberman's own notes.

1973 — reharmonization source stage IV

Killing Me Softly with His Song Roberta Flack — No. 1, Billboard Hot 100

Same melody, untouched at Stage III. Stage IV rebuilt entirely — extended jazz/soul/gospel voicings, a ii–V–I through A♭ (Fm7 → B♭m7 → E♭7 → A♭maj7), suspended dominant turnarounds. The decoder changes; the latent identity does not.

2003 — a later, contested append dispute

Authorship dispute Norman Gimbel vs. the origin story he told for three decades

No video artifact — this entry exists to keep the log honest. In the mid-2000s Gimbel moved to erase Lieberman's role in the song's origin, going as far as threatening legal action against McLean to force a retraction — after having told the Troubadour story himself, consistently, since the 1970s.

why this stays in the stream Stage I does not resolve who is right. It logs that a self-interested, later entry exists alongside the earlier corroborated ones — motive and timestamp both visible to anyone who queries it downstream.
